About the Tournament

The FIFA World Cup 2026 is the biggest in history — the first co-hosted by three nations and the first with 48 teams playing 104 matches across Canada, Mexico and the United States.

A World Cup of firsts

The 2026 edition is the 23rd FIFA World Cup and marks a series of milestones for the sport. It is the first tournament jointly hosted by three countries — Canada, Mexico and the United States — and the first to feature an expanded field of 48 nations, up from 32. The competition runs from 11 June to 19 July 2026, spanning 39 days and 104 matches.

Mexico becomes the first country to host or co-host the men's World Cup three times, having previously staged the tournaments of 1970 and 1986. The United States hosts for the first time since the record-breaking 1994 edition, while Canada stages men's World Cup matches for the very first time. Argentina arrive as the defending champions, having won the 2022 final in Qatar.

How the new format works

The 48 teams are drawn into 12 groups of four. Each side plays three group-stage matches. The top two from every group, plus the eight best third-placed teams, advance to a brand-new Round of 32 — the first knockout round in World Cup history at this stage.

Three nations, sixteen cities

Matches are spread across 16 host cities — 11 in the United States, three in Mexico and two in Canada. The tournament opens at the iconic Estadio Azteca in Mexico City on 11 June, and the final is staged at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey on 19 July.
