About the Tournament

The FIFA World Cup 2026 was the biggest in history — the first co-hosted by three nations and the first with 48 teams playing 104 matches across Canada, Mexico and the United States. Spain were crowned champions.

A World Cup of firsts

The 2026 edition was the 23rd FIFA World Cup and marked a series of milestones for the sport. It was the first tournament jointly hosted by three countries — Canada, Mexico and the United States — and the first to feature an expanded field of 48 nations, up from 32. The competition ran from 11 June to 19 July 2026, spanning 39 days and 104 matches.

Mexico became the first country to host or co-host the men's World Cup three times, having previously staged the tournaments of 1970 and 1986. The United States hosted for the first time since 1994, while Canada staged men's World Cup matches for the very first time. Argentina arrived as defending champions and reached the final again — but this time it belonged to Spain.

How it finished

Spain won their second world title, beating Argentina 1–0 after extra time in the final at MetLife Stadium on 19 July, with substitute Ferran Torres scoring in the 106th minute. England beat France 6–4 in the third-place play-off. Rodri won the Golden Ball, Kylian Mbappé the Golden Boot with 10 goals, Unai Simón the Golden Glove and Pau Cubarsí the Best Young Player award.

The tournament set records for goals (308) and total attendance (6.81 million). None of the three hosts made it past the Round of 16.

How the new format works

The 48 teams were drawn into 12 groups of four. Each side played three group-stage matches. The top two from every group, plus the eight best third-placed teams, advanced to a brand-new Round of 32 — a knockout round played at a World Cup for the first time.

Three nations, sixteen cities

Matches were spread across 16 host cities — 11 in the United States, three in Mexico and two in Canada. The tournament opened at the iconic Estadio Azteca in Mexico City on 11 June, and the final was staged at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey on 19 July.
